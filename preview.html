The question

When does a plant decide to invest in nitrogen fixation?

Agriculture leans heavily on synthetic nitrogen fertilizer. Legumes already make their own — through root nodules built in partnership with rhizobia. But a plant doesn't switch this on for free.

Building and maintaining a nitrogen-fixing nodule is costly, so the plant weighs its nutrient status, development and environment before committing. My work asks how that decision is made — and how it can be changed.

The idea shaping my programme

More nodules doesn't mean more nitrogen.

The question that matters is what makes a nodule productive, persistent and nutritionally valuable.

Formation

Can the plant build the right nodule?

Function

Can it fix nitrogen efficiently?

Longevity

Can it stay productive?

The future of legume engineering may depend less on making more nodules — and more on making better ones.

From mechanism to engineering

My research has evolved through three questions

01

Recognition

How does a legume recognise its symbiont?

Chickpea

During my PhD I characterised a family of 17 LysM receptor-like kinases and studied their role in perceiving rhizobial Nod factors — showing that knockdown of CaNFP and CaLYK3 reduces nodulation. This helped define the receptor toolkit for Nod-factor perception in a non-model crop legume.

Read the paper → Singh & Verma, Environ. Exp. Bot. 2022
02

Nutrient regulation

How does a plant decide whether it can afford symbiosis?

Common bean · phosphate signalling

In common bean, I found a direct connection between phosphate-starvation signalling and the master regulator of nodulation, NIN — showing that a plant's phosphate status can gate its decision to nodulate.

See the mechanism

The phosphate starvation–response transcription factor PvPHR-L7 binds the P1BS cis-element in the promoters of NIN and TML. Knocking it down increases nodulation; over-expressing it decreases nodulation — placing phosphate signalling directly upstream of the decision to nodulate.

PvPHR-L7 → P1BS → NIN / TML → nodulation

Read the paper → Singh et al., Plant Cell Physiol. 2025
03

Engineering

Can we engineer the system for better nitrogen fixation?

Cowpea & soybean

At the Sainsbury Laboratory I study conserved regulatory mechanisms that constrain nitrogen fixation in crop legumes — asking which genes limit nodule performance, whether those constraints can be removed, and whether regulatory pathways can be tuned rather than simply switched on or off, using CRISPR and promoter engineering.

The research programme I want to build

Engineering nutrient-smart symbiosis

A nodule is an engineered biological organ whose performance is controlled by the plant's nutritional and developmental state. I want to understand that control system — and determine whether it can be redesigned.
